Policy Risk
Residential ITC Expires End of 2025: 18% Market Contraction in 2026
Critical policy change: 30% residential solar investment tax credit (Section 25D) ended Dec 31, 2025. Originally extended to 2035, legislative changes eliminated it. Result: 18% YoY decline in residential solar installs in 2026 as financing shifts to third-party ownership (TPO) models claiming commercial tax credits. Battery attachment will rise as NEM 3.0 economics favor storage. Analysis.
Policy Impact
NEM 3.0 Upheld: Export Credits Cut 75% in California
March 2026: California Court of Appeals upheld NEM 3.0 (Net Billing Tariff) framework. Export credits reduced from $0.15–0.25/kWh (NEM 2.0) to $0.05–0.08/kWh. Payback period lengthened from 5–6 years to 9–13 years without battery. Battery storage now essential for system ROI. Enphase IQ Battery adoption accelerating. Court ruling.
Market Shift
Residential Market Pivoting to TPO (Third-Party Ownership) Models
With ITC expiring, homeowners shifting to TPO (leases, prepaid PPAs, short-term ownership transfers). TPO providers retain commercial tax credits, pass some savings to customers. Domestic content & tariff-resilient supply chains becoming procurement requirement. TPO vendor lists conservative pending domestic equipment scale-up. Impact on Enphase: B2B sales to installers/TPO providers become more critical than retail customer direct sales.
Product Trend
System Sizing Down: Smaller Systems Expected as Affordability Pressures Rise
Homebuyers purchasing based on total out-of-pocket spend. Without 30% federal tax credit, same pre-incentive system feels more expensive. Expected: smaller systems installed in 2026–2027. Component pricing rising monthly due to supply/demand imbalance. Long-term recovery forecast at 7% annual growth (2027–2030) as retail rates rise and solar costs decline.

StrategyGap Analysis: Strategic Opportunities & Risks

Product Gap: Integrated Energy Management Lag
SolarEdge's Nexis platform (launched March 2026) offers unified inverter + batteries + AI in single ecosystem. Enphase IQ Energy Management exists but less tightly integrated. Risk: SolarEdge's single-point-of-failure solution may be seen as simpler/cleaner than Enphase's modular approach. Opportunity: Emphasize Enphase's advantage in distributed architecture, no single point of failure, and superior redundancy.
Market Segment Gap: DIY/Retrofit Erosion
APsystems EZ1-LV plug-in microinverter now available direct to consumers in U.S. (Feb 2026). Targets homeowners who want to avoid professional installation. Enphase has limited direct-to-consumer DIY product. Risk: Losing retrofit and self-install market. Opportunity: Develop or acquire plug-and-play residential product for DIY segment; partner with online retailers (Amazon, Home Depot, etc.).
Geographic Gap: Emerging Market Competition
Hoymiles secured 360 MW India deal (Kosol Energie, Jan 2026)—largest single microinverter order in Indian market. Hoymiles also expanding Brazil. Enphase's India footprint less visible. Risk: Losing scale advantage in fast-growing emerging markets where price competition intense. Opportunity: Aggressive India/Brazil expansion partnerships with local distributors; competitive pricing for high-volume orders.
Policy Navigation Gap: TPO Model Readiness
18% residential market contraction in 2026 as customers shift from direct purchase to TPO (leases, prepaid PPAs). TPO providers have conservative vendor lists awaiting domestic supply scale-up. Risk: Enphase's growth dependent on residential retail sales; TPO channel development slower than expected. Opportunity: Direct B2B sales to major TPO operators (Sunrun, Vivint, etc.); ensure Enphase systems on approved vendor lists; emphasize U.S. manufacturing for domestic content advantage.
Warranty/Perception Gap: Tesla Powerwall Safety Concerns
Tesla Powerwall facing safety recalls (10K+ units). FranklinWH aPower 2 and EcoFlow gaining mindshare. Enphase IQ Battery positioned with LFP (safer than ternary chemistry) but brand awareness low. Risk: Customers still perceive Powerwall as default battery choice despite safety issues. Opportunity: Marketing campaign emphasizing LFP safety, 15-year warranty, AI energy management, and Enphase ecosystem integration. Highlight absence of recalls and superior thermal stability.
Commercial Market Gap: GaN Microinverter Adoption
Enphase IQ9N-3P (GaN-based, 480V three-phase, 97.5% efficiency, no external transformer) is innovative and differentiated. However, market awareness limited. Power Control software reduces permitting complexity—strong value proposition. Risk: Competitors (e.g., SMA) may also launch GaN products; adoption pace uncertain. Opportunity: Aggressive marketing to commercial installers in California and high-permitting-cost states; partner with commercial TPOs; case studies of permitting time/cost reductions.
